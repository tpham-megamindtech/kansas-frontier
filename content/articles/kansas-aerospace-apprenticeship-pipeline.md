Faced with an aging workforce and persistent demand, Kansas aerospace manufacturers are partnering with community colleges and the state to build an apprenticeship pipeline that trains the next generation of aircraft assemblers, machinists, and technicians.

The programs blend paid on-the-job training with classroom instruction, allowing students to earn while they learn and step into skilled jobs without a mountain of debt. Wichita's technical training center has become a key feeder, turning out workers with the specialized skills the industry requires.

"You cannot build an airplane without people who know how to build an airplane, and those people take years to train," an industry workforce official said. Employers say the investment is essential to sustaining production.

State leaders have backed the effort with funding, viewing aerospace as a strategic industry whose future depends on a steady supply of skilled labor.
