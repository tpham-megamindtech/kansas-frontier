A wave of new wind energy projects broke ground across Kansas this winter, part of a record investment cycle that is reinforcing the state's position among the nation's top wind-power producers.

Kansas already generates a large share of its electricity from wind, and the flat, gusty expanses of the western half of the state have made it a magnet for developers. The newly announced projects, spread across several counties, are expected to funnel lease payments to farmers and property tax revenue to rural school districts.

"For a lot of these landowners, a row of turbines is the most reliable crop they have ever had," said an analyst with a Wichita-based energy consultancy. Wind lease payments have become a meaningful supplement to farm income in an era of volatile commodity prices.

### Grid questions remain

The buildout has renewed debate over transmission capacity, as the existing grid struggles to carry power from remote wind farms to population centers. Utility planners say new high-voltage lines will be needed to unlock the full potential of the resource. For now, though, the cranes and turbine blades rising over the plains are a visible sign of money flowing into rural Kansas.
