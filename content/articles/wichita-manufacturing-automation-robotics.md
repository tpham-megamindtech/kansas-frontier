Manufacturers across Wichita are integrating robotics and automation into their production lines, aiming to boost productivity and quality while redeploying workers to higher-skilled tasks.

Robots now handle repetitive, precise, or physically demanding jobs, from drilling and fastening to material handling, working alongside human employees. Manufacturers say automation helps them compete globally and offsets the tight labor market, rather than simply replacing workers.

"Automation lets us put people where human judgment actually matters," a plant manager said. The shift requires workers skilled in operating and maintaining the equipment, changing the nature of factory jobs.

Labor advocates urge attention to retraining, while manufacturers argue that automation is essential to keeping production, and the jobs that surround it, in Kansas.
