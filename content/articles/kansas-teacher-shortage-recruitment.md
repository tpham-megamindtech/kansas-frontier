As another school year approached, districts across Kansas scrambled to fill classroom vacancies, deploying an array of new tactics to counter a teacher shortage that has hit rural and urban schools alike.

Some districts have launched grow-your-own programs that help paraprofessionals and high school graduates earn teaching credentials while working locally. Others are offering signing bonuses, help with student loans, or even subsidized housing in communities where rentals are scarce.

"We cannot out-spend the big suburban districts, so we have to be creative and we have to be personal," a superintendent in central Kansas said. State officials have expanded alternative licensing pathways in response.

Education advocates warn that pay remains the central issue, and that incentives can only go so far without broader increases in teacher compensation.
