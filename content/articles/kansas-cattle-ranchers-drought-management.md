Ranchers across western Kansas are rethinking how many cattle their land can carry, as recurring drought reshapes the range and forces hard decisions about herd size and grazing.

The cattle business is a cornerstone of the Kansas economy, and the state's grasslands, particularly the Flint Hills, are prized for fattening steers on nutritious native grass. But a run of dry years has thinned pastures in places, prompting some operators to cull herds early or lease additional ground far from home.

"You can always buy more cattle. You cannot easily buy back a pasture you have overgrazed," a rancher near the Colorado line said. Extension specialists are promoting rotational grazing and flexible stocking rates to protect the grass in lean years.

Ranchers who manage their forage carefully, experts say, are better positioned to weather the dry cycles that have always been part of life on the High Plains.
