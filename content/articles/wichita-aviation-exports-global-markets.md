Exports remain vital to Wichita's aircraft manufacturers, who sell a substantial share of their planes to customers around the world, making the health of the local industry deeply tied to global markets.

Business jets and general aviation aircraft built in Kansas find buyers across Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America. That international reach fuels the region's economy but also exposes it to currency swings, trade policy, and economic conditions far from the plains.

"An airplane built in Wichita might end up on a runway on the other side of the planet," a trade official said. Manufacturers maintain global sales and support networks to serve far-flung customers.

Industry leaders warn that trade barriers or tariffs could hit the export-dependent sector hard, and they closely watch international negotiations that could affect their markets.
