Administrators from a group of rural Kansas hospitals delivered a stark warning to a legislative committee this week, telling lawmakers that razor-thin operating margins and persistent staffing shortages could force some facilities to cut services or close entirely.

Kansas has one of the higher counts of critical-access hospitals in the country, small facilities that serve as the only source of emergency and inpatient care for wide swaths of the state. Several have already suspended labor and delivery units, leaving expectant mothers to drive an hour or more.

"When a rural hospital closes, the whole town's economy takes the hit, not just its health," one administrator testified. Hospital leaders asked lawmakers to consider a stabilization fund and changes to how the state reimburses care.

Legislators from both parties expressed sympathy, though they cautioned that competing budget priorities will make new spending difficult.
