The booth rental model is turning a growing number of Kansas stylists into independent small-business owners, allowing them to set their own hours, prices, and brand while working within an established salon.

Under the arrangement, stylists rent a station rather than working as employees, taking on the responsibilities and rewards of running their own operation. Many say the independence is liberating, though it also means handling their own marketing, supplies, and taxes.

"You are your own boss, but you are also your own everything else," one Wichita stylist said. Salon owners benefit from steady rental income and a roster of motivated professionals.

Business advisers encourage stylists considering the leap to plan carefully, build a client base first, and understand the financial responsibilities of self-employment.
