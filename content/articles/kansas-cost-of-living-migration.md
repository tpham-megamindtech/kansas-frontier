Kansas economic developers are leaning into the state's affordability, marketing low housing costs and short commutes to workers and companies weighing an escape from pricier metropolitan areas on the coasts.

Recruitment campaigns highlight the gap in home prices between Kansas cities and places like Denver, Chicago, or the West Coast, arguing that a paycheck stretches further on the plains. Some communities have gone further, offering relocation incentives to remote workers.

"You can own a home here on a salary that would barely cover rent somewhere else," a development official said. Whether affordability alone can reverse decades of slow population growth remains an open question.

Skeptics note that jobs, schools, and amenities matter as much as cost, and that Kansas competes with equally affordable neighboring states for the same workers.
