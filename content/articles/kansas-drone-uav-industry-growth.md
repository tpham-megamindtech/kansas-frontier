Kansas is positioning itself at the forefront of drone technology, leveraging its wide-open airspace, deep aviation roots, and research institutions to become a proving ground for commercial unmanned aircraft.

Researchers and companies are testing drones for agriculture, infrastructure inspection, and package delivery, taking advantage of Kansas's sparse airspace and supportive regulatory partnerships. The state's aviation heritage provides a base of engineering talent and manufacturing know-how.

"Unmanned aircraft are the next chapter of aviation, and Kansas intends to write part of it," a researcher said. Universities have established programs to study drone operations and train operators.

Advocates say the sector could create new jobs and applications, though it faces challenges around regulation, airspace integration, and public acceptance.
