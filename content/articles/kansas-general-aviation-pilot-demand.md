A global shortage of pilots is driving strong demand for training aircraft and flight instruction, a boon for Kansas's general aviation sector and the manufacturers that build small planes.

Airlines and cargo carriers need thousands of new pilots as veterans retire and air travel grows, and that demand cascades down to flight schools and the makers of training aircraft. Kansas, with its manufacturing base and ample airspace, is well positioned to benefit.

"Every new airline pilot started in a small airplane, and a lot of those airplanes are built here," an aviation official said. Flight schools report full rosters and waiting lists.

The training pipeline has become a strategic concern for the entire aviation industry, and Kansas manufacturers see steady demand for the light aircraft that launch aviation careers.
