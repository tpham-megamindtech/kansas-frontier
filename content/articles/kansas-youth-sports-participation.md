Communities across Kansas are investing in youth sports facilities, building fields, courts, and multi-sport complexes to serve growing leagues and, in many cases, to attract tournament visitors.

The complexes host baseball, softball, soccer, and other sports, giving local children places to play while drawing traveling teams whose families fill hotels and restaurants. Officials increasingly view youth sports tourism as an economic development strategy as well as a community amenity.

"These facilities keep our kids active and bring visitors to town on the weekends," a city official said. The tournaments can provide a meaningful boost to local businesses.

Advocates for youth sports welcome the investment while cautioning about the rising cost and competitiveness of organized athletics, urging communities to keep opportunities accessible to all families.
