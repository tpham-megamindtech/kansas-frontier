Federally backed crop insurance remains the financial backbone of Kansas agriculture, providing a safety net that farmers and their lenders say makes it possible to keep planting in the face of relentless weather risk.

In a state where a single hailstorm or drought can wipe out a season, insurance coverage often determines whether an operation survives a bad year. Bankers frequently require it as a condition of lending, and policymakers treat the program as a linchpin of farm policy.

"Without crop insurance, a lot of these farms simply could not get financing," a farm lender said. Debates in Washington over the program's cost and structure are watched closely across rural Kansas.

Agricultural economists note that insurance manages risk but does not eliminate it, and that repeated losses still erode the equity farms need to keep going.
