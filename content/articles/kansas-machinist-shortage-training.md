A shortage of skilled machinists is prompting Kansas manufacturers and technical schools to ramp up training for one of industry's most essential and enduring trades.

Machinists, who cut and shape metal parts to exacting tolerances, are the backbone of the state's aerospace and manufacturing sectors. But many veteran machinists are retiring, and employers say too few young people are entering the field, despite good pay and steady demand.

"You can build a whole career, and a good living, running these machines," a shop instructor said. Modern machining increasingly involves computer-controlled equipment, blending hands-on skill with programming.

Schools have modernized their programs and are working to change perceptions of manufacturing careers, hoping to convince students and parents that the trade offers a bright future.
